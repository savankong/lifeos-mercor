Executive Summary

The short version — see the full vault for sourced detail.

Fastest grower, on the back of one shock

Mercor's ARR climbed from ~$75M (Feb 2025) to a reported ~$2B run-rate (mid-2026), largely tracking the window after Meta's $14.3B stake in Scale AI (Jun 2025) broke Scale's neutrality and sent lab spend to "neutral" vendors. Most of Mercor's growth is market reallocation, not proven durable share.

Mercor's actual edge

Not raw scale — Surge AI and Turing are comparably or more capital-efficient. Mercor's differentiated claim is the credentialed-expert niche (doctors, lawyers, PhDs) plus an AI-vetting/matching flywheel. Micro1 runs a near-identical model at ~1/7th the scale, the best available read on whether this is company-specific or category-wide.

Two live risk flags

A March 2026 supply-chain breach (poisoned LiteLLM package) cost Mercor its Meta contract and exposed up to 4TB of data. Separately, CEO Foody personally invested in Deeptune before having Mercor acquire it three months later — a governance flag, not an allegation of wrongdoing.

Field is more contested than the headlines suggest

Scale AI's own 2026 revenue guidance (~$1B, down from ~$2B in 2025) is the clearest quantified evidence of value transferred to challengers — but Scale isn't exiting, it's pivoting to enterprise deployment (Mayo Clinic, Pentagon cited). Six credible players are now splitting frontier-lab budgets.

The credential-tier players (Mercor, Surge, Micro1) genuinely compete for the same open-market pool of professionals and will feel each other's pricing pressure directly. Handshake and Turing aren't drawing from that pool at all — each has captured a structurally different labor market, which makes them look less like head-to-head Mercor competitors and more like parallel bets on different talent supplies entirely.

Corrected ranking

Mercor and Scale AI tie for clearest entry point — both publish explicit dollar figures and a defined intake process; Scale's is arguably more concrete ($10K–$1M+ range vs. Mercor's referral-only figure). Handshake is real but price-opaque — more friction than it first appears. Surge AI is not self-serve — a claimed "Consulting Program" for pipelines of experts does not appear to exist; engaging them means cold outreach with no published terms.

Three ways to sell in

1) Sell operational data directly (the Mercor/Scale/Handshake model). 2) Sell a pipeline of vetted expertise — originate access to a specific professional population, the same mechanic as the core marketplaces. 3) Broker/originate for a fee — the lowest-capital option, validated by Mercor's and Scale's real referral programs, though the fee is a small fraction of full deal value.

Mercor — Business Model Deep Dive

Unit economics, go-to-market, the full product portfolio, and what Mercor itself chooses to publicize as its headline metrics.

Unit economics: a ~35% take-rate labor marketplace

Take rate~35% of gross payment volume
Contractor share60–70% of client billing
Rate card range$65–$150+/hr by domain
Implied net revenue~$600–700M/yr*

*This vault's own estimate: applying the verified ~35% take rate to the ~$2B gross run-rate. Most press headlines cite the gross figure — a materially different (larger) number than what Mercor actually keeps. This margin profile is in line with BPO (business-process-outsourcing) medians, not typical SaaS gross margins — a real framing question for how the company should be valued.

Go-to-market: different motion per product line

Core marketplace: historically no dedicated sales team — "there isn't a single person who works on sales at Mercor outside of the founders." Growth came from word-of-mouth and placement-quality reputation; candidate acquisition via free interview/resume tools, referrals, university outreach, and GitHub scraping.

Enterprise AI (new): conventional high-touch B2B — no public pricing, "book a demo," targeting 1,000+-employee organizations and PE due-diligence teams.

Data monetization (new): referral-driven — up to $100K referral bonuses paid to enterprises that bring in data-licensing deals.

Four product lines, not one

Post-Training Data Suite

RLHF, SFT, rubrics, RL environments, plus the APEX benchmark family (now including APEX-Agents, APEX-Accounting, APEX-SWE). The original and still-dominant revenue line.

Mercor Enterprise AI

Forward-Deployed-Engineer-style consulting: Discover → Deploy → Improve → Monetize. Published case-study claims: "50%+ reduction in time to incident mitigation," "$7–9M upside savings identified," "$20M annual run-rate impact by 2031," "~12,000 support requests/week handled."

Mercor Data

Pays enterprises to anonymize and license internal Slack/email/CRM/code/finance data to AI labs. Claims SOC 2 Type II, 60+ sensitive-identifier detection, 50+ integrations — claims worth extra scrutiny given Mercor's own March 2026 breach.

Mercor Voice / Vetting Engine

The AI-interview and candidate-screening infrastructure underneath every other product line — 468,000+ applicants evaluated, 30,000+ active experts. Mercor's core defensibility claim, not independently audited.

What Mercor tracks publicly

As of August 2026, mercor.com's own homepage displays three live counters as its headline public KPIs: Average contracted rate ($/hr), Roles created (k), and Daily payouts ($). That choice is itself a signal — Mercor markets marketplace liquidity and payout velocity ("real money moving to real experts, right now"), not SaaS-style metrics like net revenue retention or logo count. Analysts and press separately reconstruct: take rate (~35%), gross margin (33% in Q2 2026, guided to ~46%), revenue per US employee (~$1.7M at the $50M-ARR stage), and customer concentration (~91% of H1 2026 revenue from foundation-model labs).

Risk Flags

The two events most relevant to any durability assessment of Mercor specifically.

March 2026 — Supply-chain data breach

A poisoned version of the open-source LiteLLM package, published to PyPI by threat actor "TeamPCP," harvested credentials for ~40 minutes and reportedly exposed up to 4TB of internal data — candidate PII, employer data, source code, API keys. Meta paused its Mercor contracts indefinitely. OpenAI investigated exposure but had not terminated as of reporting. A class-action lawsuit reportedly followed, affecting 40,000+ people.

July 2026 — Deeptune acquisition, governance flag

CEO Brendan Foody personally angel-invested in Deeptune's $43M Series A (~April 2026), then had Mercor acquire Deeptune three months later. He told Fortune the personal investment was "in a lot of ways the main motivation" for backing Deeptune early. Not illegal or confirmed improper — but an insider-positioning pattern worth tracking, especially during a reported $20B fundraise.

November 2025 — Contractor pay-cut incident ("Musen" → "Nova")

A project reviewing Meta Reels content ("Musen") was abruptly cancelled — contractors locked out of Slack — then replaced hours later with a new project ("Nova") offered at $16/hr, $5 below Musen's $21/hr, with some contractors having earned up to $60/hr on prior Mercor work. Mercor disputes the "wage cut" characterization, framing it as normal project-based reallocation. The first concrete, dated instance of the broader "declining pay" pattern referenced in press coverage — and a reminder that contractor-rate volatility is structural to this entire business model, not unique to Mercor.

Ongoing — Customer concentration

~91% of H1 2026 revenue came from AI foundation-model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind named). If labs build internal data-ops teams — several are known to be doing so — Mercor's core market could compress. Gross margin was 33% in Q2 2026 (contractors take 60–70% of top-line revenue), with guidance to ~46%.

Market Trends

CriticalHigh

The neutrality shift

Meta's 49% stake in Scale AI (Jun 2025) made Scale commercially untenable for rival labs as a data vendor. Google, OpenAI, and xAI reportedly cut or reduced ties within weeks — the single biggest external driver of growth across this entire competitive set.

HighConflict

Market growth & data scarcity

AI training-dataset market: $4.44B in 2026 → $23.18B by 2034 (22.9% CAGR, Fortune Business Insights — verified). Other firms estimate 2–3x higher, reflecting a genuine scope disagreement (raw datasets vs. broader human-labor spend). Quality public text is running thin, pushing labs toward expert-generated data.

HighHigh

Security & trust risk

Trust — not just cost or quality — is now the primary competitive currency. Two shocks in twelve months (Scale's ownership conflict, Mercor's breach) show any vendor's moat now rests on demonstrable independence and demonstrable security hygiene.
