\# Innodata Partnership Angles

**Pass 13 — 2026-08-16**
**Context:** the requester has a personal relationship at Innodata and wants concrete angles to explore a business partnership, rather than treating Innodata purely as a competitor. See `Companies/Innodata.md` for the full research this is built on.

## Why Innodata specifically, out of everyone in this vault

Two things make Innodata a better-fit conversation than Mercor, Scale AI, Surge AI, or Handshake AI: **(1)** it already runs a named external-contributor channel — the "SME on Demand" program — so there's an existing door to walk through rather than a cold pitch for a program that doesn't exist; **(2)** it explicitly states it maintains **security-cleared federal teams**, which is a capability none of the other four companies show any public evidence of (`YOUR-ROSTER-GOVERNMENT-DEMAND-ANALYSIS.md` found zero government/defense category on Mercor's or Handshake's public marketplaces). If the government/defense angle from earlier passes has any life left in it, Innodata — not Mercor or Handshake — is the company that already has the compliance infrastructure to act on it.

## Five angles, ordered by how directly they're supported by what's verified

### 1. SME-on-Demand supply partner (strongest, most direct)

Innodata's own SME-on-Demand program is a real, named channel for external domain experts. Pitch: **"We can supply vetted [specific domain] professionals into your SME-on-Demand pipeline, especially for surge capacity as you scale the new Big Tech engagements."** This is directly relevant given Innodata's own stated 2026 priority (diversifying customers *and* "broadening... the number of programs it supports" per its Q2 earnings call) — they need more supply capacity to service growth, not less.

**Caveat to raise yourself, not have raised at you:** Innodata's base annotation tier pays ~$9/hr — far below what credentialed experts command elsewhere in this market (Mercor: $85–150+/hr). Position explicitly for the SME/domain-expert tier, and say so directly in the conversation, or risk the whole relationship getting anchored to the low-end rate card.

### 2. The cleared-federal-teams / government angle (highest differentiation, needs a real check first)

If there's a genuine network of former government/defense/cleared professionals to draw on, this is the one place in the entire competitive set with a demonstrated, existing channel for it. Pitch: **"Given your cleared federal teams, is there unmet demand for [specific unclassified government-process expertise / cleared domain expertise] you're currently capacity-constrained on?"** Ask this as a real, open question — don't presuppose the answer. `YOUR-ROSTER-GOVERNMENT-DEMAND-ANALYSIS.md` found real demand signals (both frontier labs directly hire cleared policy/national-security specialists at high salaries) but no evidence of a scalable marketplace anywhere — Innodata's cleared-team infrastructure is the one plausible exception worth testing directly rather than assuming.

### 3. Warm-intro / BD engine for Innodata's own sales motion (different value prop entirely)

Your Roster's core technology is mapping a network to warm-intro paths into companies. Innodata's own stated 2026 priority is landing *new* frontier-lab and enterprise logos to reduce customer concentration (down from 56% to 37% on its top account, but still actively working the problem). Pitch: **use Your Roster's own matching engine as a sales-enablement tool for Innodata's BD team** — surfacing warm paths into prospective AI-lab/enterprise customers Innodata wants to reach. This isn't a data-supply relationship at all — it's selling Your Roster's actual product to Innodata as a customer, which sidesteps every data-consent/brokerage complication raised in Passes 9–12 entirely. Worth floating as a distinct option, not a fallback.

### 4. Vertical-specific sourcing tied to named Innodata products

Innodata explicitly names healthcare, finance, and legal SME domains, and runs a dedicated medical-record platform (**Synodex**). If Your Roster's actual user base has real concentration in any of these verticals (something worth checking in your own signup data before pitching), that's a second, more specific version of Angle 1: **"We have unusual depth in [healthcare/finance/legal] — is there a fit with Synodex or your SME-on-Demand pipeline specifically there?"**

### 5. Pre-launch positioning on the "GenAI Platform (Coming Soon)"

Innodata's own site lists a GenAI platform as an upcoming product (SFT, prompt writing, response annotation, RLHF evaluation). Asking directly what expertise/supply gaps that platform will need, before it launches, is a time-sensitive way to get ahead of Mercor/Surge/Micro1 on a specific new surface rather than competing for share of an already-established one.

## The one question to ask before any of the above, regardless of which angle lands

Same principle as every other buyer conversation in this vault: **ask what they can't currently source, don't lead with what you're offering.** Innodata is large enough (11,500+ in-house SMEs already) that the real opportunity is whatever specific gap exists despite that scale — likely a narrow vertical, a surge-capacity crunch tied to the new $51M account, or the cleared-federal-teams angle specifically, not a generic "we have experts too" pitch.

## What this doesn't establish

- No confirmation Innodata pays external SME-on-Demand contributors at rates comparable to Mercor/Surge, or what its actual margin/take structure looks like — unlike Mercor's and Scale AI's publicly quantified referral programs, Innodata discloses nothing about SME-on-Demand compensation.
- No confirmation of genuine unmet demand in any specific vertical — Angles 1, 2, and 4 are informed hypotheses to test in conversation, not validated gaps.
- No assessment of whether Your Roster's actual user base has the concentration needed to make Angle 4 credible — that requires looking at real signup data, not vault research.
