\# Your Roster — First-Party Expert-Data Monetization Strategy

**Status:** STRATEGY (not yet built or validated)
**Pass 9 — 2026-08-16**
**Supersedes:** the connections-data-resale idea raised earlier in this conversation, which I flagged as a likely LinkedIn ToS violation and a third-party-consent problem (the user's *connections* never agreed to anything). This document is the consented, first-party alternative: Your Roster acquires website visitors, and — with clear, separate, paid consent — has them contribute their own professional judgment/expertise, not other people's data. That's a real, existing product category: it's what Mercor, Surge AI, and Micro1's entire business is built on (see `LANDSCAPE.md`'s talent-segmentation table), and it's a close extension of Your Roster's actual user base (5+ years experience, 500+ connections — the same "credentialed mid-career professional" segment those companies recruit).

## Two paths, not one — pick a starting point deliberately

### Path A — Sourcing/referral partner (fast, low-build, start here)

Your Roster already has exactly the audience Mercor/Surge/Micro1/Handshake want as **contractors**, not just job-board applicants. Rather than building new data-collection infrastructure, the fast version of this is: surface these companies' *expert/contractor* opportunities (not just their W2 job listings) to matching users, and refer them in.

- **What it takes to build:** almost nothing new — it's the existing warm-intro/job-matching pipeline, pointed at a different type of opportunity (contractor gigs at Mercor/Surge/Micro1/Handshake, alongside the W2 roles already in scope from `YOUR-ROSTER-OPPORTUNITY.md`).
- **Revenue mechanism — be precise about what's actually verified:** Mercor's and Scale AI's published referral bonuses (up to $100K and $10K respectively) are for **enterprise data-partnership introductions**, not individual-candidate referrals — I don't have a verified public program for "refer an individual expert candidate, earn a fee" at any of these companies. Mercor's own placement-fee model (~30% of first-year comp, or the hourly marked-up-rate model) is charged to the *client* (the AI lab), not paid out to a third-party referrer, as far as this vault's research has found. **This means Path A's revenue would most likely require negotiating an actual sourcing/channel-partner agreement directly with one of these companies (Mercor is the standout candidate given its recruiting-marketplace origins) — not a self-serve link you can start using today.** Worth a direct conversation before building around an assumed fee structure. Yang Zhao at Handshake AI (`People/Yang-Zhao.md`) or Mercor's own BD channel (`SELLING-STRATEGY.md`) are the right entry points to ask directly what a sourcing-partner arrangement would look like.
- **Risk profile:** low. No new data-collection consent model needed, no new legal exposure, validates whether this user base actually wants contractor gig work before you build anything bigger.

### Path B — Build a proprietary expert-response dataset (bigger opportunity, bigger lift)

This is what "generate data to sell directly to Mercor/Handshake/Anthropic/OpenAI" actually requires if you mean a data *product*, not a referral fee:

1. **Design a consented, compensated data-collection flow**, clearly separate from the core job-search product. Users need to explicitly opt into a *different* thing than warm-intro matching — something like: "Answer structured questions in your area of expertise. You'll be paid per response. Your responses may be licensed to AI companies for model training/evaluation, in de-identified form." This needs to be an affirmative, separate consent step — not a checkbox buried in onboarding — both because it's the right thing to do and because every verified buyer program in this vault (Mercor, Scale AI, Handshake) explicitly requires the *data provider* to warrant they have the rights to share what they're selling. If Your Roster can't show clean, informed consent from each contributor, no legitimate buyer's compliance team will sign off on the deal.
2. **What "the information" should actually be** — not résumés or connections, but domain-expert output: professional-judgment scenarios, rubric-based evaluations of hypothetical work product, reasoning traces ("how would you approach X problem in your field"), red-teaming/quality review of AI outputs in the user's domain. This is literally the RLHF/SFT/rubrics category documented in `Products/Mercor-Post-Training-Suite.md` — Your Roster would be building a smaller, audience-specific version of what Mercor already runs at scale.
3. **Pick a wedge vertical, don't go broad on day one.** Mercor/Surge/Micro1 already cover generalist credentialed-professional demand at massive scale — Your Roster competing head-on there with no infrastructure is a weak position. The better opening move is a vertical where Your Roster's actual user base is unusually concentrated or differentiated (whatever that turns out to be from real signup data) — a specific professional niche is a much stronger pitch to a buyer than "we also have some experts," and it's the only way this path outcompetes just referring users into Mercor's existing, much larger, already-trusted marketplace (Path A).
4. **Who to actually approach, and in what order, once a real dataset/pilot exists:**
   - **Mercor** and **Scale AI** have the clearest, most concretely quantified data-partnership programs (see `SELLING-STRATEGY.md`) — but both are scoped around *enterprise operational data* (Slack/CRM/docs), not third-party-aggregated expert-response datasets. A pitch here would need to go in as a direct BD conversation, not through the published intake form as-is.
   - **Handshake AI** is arguably the closer structural match, since its entire model *is* buying human expert judgment at scale from a network it doesn't directly employ — closer to what Your Roster would be offering than Mercor's or Scale's enterprise-data programs are. **Yang Zhao (CBO)** is the best-verified contact for this conversation (`People/Yang-Zhao.md`).
   - **Anthropic and OpenAI directly:** no verified public vendor-data-purchase program was found for either in this vault's research (unlike Mercor/Scale/Handshake, which all publish one). The realistic path to their training pipelines is very likely *through* an intermediary like Mercor, Surge, or Handshake — who already have those lab relationships — rather than a cold, direct pitch to a frontier lab. Treat "sell directly to Anthropic/OpenAI" as a later-stage ambition, not a first move.
5. **Risk profile:** meaningfully higher than Path A. Real legal work needed (consent language, data licensing terms, contributor compensation structure, de-identification if any personal detail is embedded in responses) before approaching any buyer — none of this exists yet. Also directly competitive with Mercor/Surge/Micro1, not complementary to them, which changes the relationship with those companies from "potential partner" to "potential competitor" in any BD conversation.

## Recommended sequencing

Start with **Path A** as a fast, low-risk test of whether Your Roster's users actually want contractor/expert gig work surfaced alongside job listings — it reuses the existing product, costs almost nothing extra to build, and produces a real signal (does anyone click through and apply?) before any data-product investment. If that validates and reveals a clear vertical concentration in your user base, **Path B** becomes a much better-supported pitch — to Handshake AI first, given the structural fit — rather than a generic "we have some experts" cold outreach.

## What this document does not do

- It does not draft actual website copy or a consent-flow UI — that's a next step once a path is chosen, not something to build speculatively.
- It does not confirm Mercor, Scale AI, or Handshake AI would actually want to buy a Your-Roster-branded dataset — all three companies' verified programs are scoped for different data types (enterprise ops data, not third-party-aggregated expert responses); this would need a direct conversation, not an assumption.
- It does not address the legal/licensing work (consent language, compensation terms, data rights) required before Path B could ethically or safely proceed — that needs actual legal review, not vault research.
