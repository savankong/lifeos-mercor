## What the memo actually says

- **Stage:** pre-seed.
- **Positioning:** "transitioning the AI training-data market toward quality over quantity."
- **Core value proposition:** *"LinkedIn can tell you who your connections are. It cannot tell you who to ask."* Your Roster creates warm introductions by analyzing verified network connections against open positions — the mechanic this vault had already researched accurately in Passes 8–9.
- **Dual revenue model:** a free job-matching product (user acquisition) plus paid AI training/evaluation work for frontier labs (monetization), running on **one shared verification layer** — the memo's own phrase: *"one verification layer, two ways of getting paid."* This reconciles what looked in this conversation like two competing directions (warm-intro job platform vs. data-seller pivot) — per the founder's own framing, they were never actually in tension. They're the same trust graph monetized twice.
- **Trust architecture — the actual wedge, previously unresearched:** credential verification through **named colleagues**, not AI scoring or self-reported claims, with graded verification labels from "independently verified" to "inferred." This is a materially different and more specific mechanism than anything this vault had attributed to Your Roster before this pass, and it directly answers the "no identified wedge yet" gap flagged in `YOUR-ROSTER-DATA-BUSINESS-ASSESSMENT.md` — the wedge is the verification mechanism itself, not just access to a particular professional population.
- **Initial target market / wedge strategy:** **federal employees transitioning from government, and GovCon contractors** — leveraging existing security clearances and dense agency alumni networks. Addressable population: **~1.3–5.1 million cleared professionals** across government and contractor sectors.
- **Founding team:** **Savan Kong** (CEO — **former DoD Customer Experience Officer**), **Matt Kelly** (Growth co-founder), **Dave Spellman** (COO, 20+ years scaling early-stage companies).
- **Financial model:** 32.5% marketplace take rate on verified placements; illustrative math shows ~$500K ARR requiring roughly 15–20 concurrent engagements.

## Reconciling this against what this vault had assumed or drafted earlier

Worth being precise about what changes and what doesn't, rather than silently overwriting Passes 11–14:

- **The government wedge is now grounded, not illustrative.** This vault's Pass 5 example (a hypothetical network of "former DoD program managers") was flagged repeatedly as this vault's own illustrative construction, not evidence. The funding memo shows the real basis was always there — the CEO's own background as a former DoD Customer Experience Officer — it just hadn't been surfaced in this conversation until now. Pass 12's structural concerns (clearance-gated work doesn't fit an open marketplace model; established vendors like Scale AI already hold major DoD contracts) still apply and aren't resolved by this memo — they're the real risks a "federal employees + GovCon contractors" go-to-market will run into, not concerns this new information erases.
- **"High-net-worth former government officials"** (the framing used in the Pass 14 Innodata outreach email) is **narrower and different** from what the memo actually describes — the memo's addressable population is **federal employees and GovCon contractors broadly** (1.3–5.1M people), not specifically senior/wealthy former officials. Both framings may have a place (a broad addressable market vs. a narrower high-value beachhead for an initial pitch), but they shouldn't be conflated as the same claim — the Innodata email was written before this memo was shared with this vault, and its "high-net-worth" language was the requester's direction at the time, not sourced from this document.
- **Healthcare is new** — not mentioned in any prior pass. Added here as the second high-compliance vertical, per direct instruction, and it has real supporting evidence already in this vault: Mercor's live job board lists healthcare-specific roles (Medicare Advantage evaluation among them), and Innodata explicitly names healthcare as an SME domain and runs a dedicated medical-record platform (Synodex, see `Companies/Innodata.md`). Government and healthcare share the property that makes them a coherent "high-compliance industries" wedge rather than two unrelated bets: both run on formal, verifiable credentialing (clearances; licensure/board certification) that maps directly onto Your Roster's named-colleague verification mechanism.
