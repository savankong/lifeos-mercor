## Overview

Innodata (NASDAQ: INOD) is a publicly traded, decades-old data-engineering company that pivoted hard into AI data services — annotation, evaluation, RLHF/DPO, red-teaming, RL environments, agentic AI development, and more. Structurally closer to Invisible Technologies (older BPO-style company, not a 2023-era marketplace startup) than to Mercor/Surge, but with a much broader product portfolio: **16 distinct service lines** were found on its own site, including a medical-record-extraction platform (Synodex), a PR/GenAI product (Agility PR Solutions), document intelligence, and physical AI/computer vision — a genuine enterprise data-engineering conglomerate, not a single-product marketplace. *(HIGH — verified via direct fetch of innodata.com)*

## Scale & Financials

- Revenue growth guided at **~40%+ YoY for 2026**.
- **Customer concentration was severe and is now improving fast**: largest customer was ~58% of 2025 revenue and 56% of Q1 2026 revenue — dropped to **37%** by Q2 2026, with a second big-tech customer scaling to **34%**. A new Big Tech engagement is expected to generate **~$51M in 2026**, likely becoming its second-largest account. Management explicitly frames customer diversification as a current strategic priority.
- **5 of the "Magnificent 7" are customers.**
- New partnership with **Palantir** (Jan 29, 2026) for data engineering/annotation supporting Palantir's AI platform deployments (event analytics) — a notable overlap with `YOUR-ROSTER-GOVERNMENT-DEMAND-ANALYSIS.md`, since Palantir is also central to Anthropic's DoD/defense workflow integration researched there.

## Talent/Workforce Model — the key finding that changes the "partner vs. competitor" read

Innodata runs a real, named **"Subject Matter Expert (SME) on Demand"** program — an external-contributor pipeline, contradicting this vault's initial (incorrect) read that Innodata had no open expert-sourcing channel. Claims **11,500+ in-house subject matter experts** across STEM, healthcare, finance, and legal, operating from **20+ global delivery locations in 120+ languages**. Critically, it also states it maintains **security-cleared federal teams** — a capability neither Mercor nor Handshake AI shows any public evidence of (see `YOUR-ROSTER-GOVERNMENT-DEMAND-ANALYSIS.md` finding #5, where both companies' public marketplaces excluded government/defense entirely). *(HIGH — verified via direct fetch of innodata.com)*

**Important pay-tier caveat:** base "Data Annotation Specialist" roles found on Innodata's job board pay around **$9/hour** — far below Mercor's $85–150+/hr credentialed-expert rates. Innodata's workforce appears to span a much wider quality/pay spectrum than Mercor's uniformly high-end marketplace — from low-cost generalist annotation up to the named SME-on-demand and cleared-federal tiers. Any partnership pitch needs to target the SME/cleared tier explicitly, not the generalist annotation tier, to avoid being commoditized into the $9/hr bucket. *(HIGH — verified via direct job-posting search)*

## Is it a partner or a competitor?

**Competitor to the core business** — it sells the same annotation/RLHF/evaluation/red-teaming services to the same buyer set (frontier labs, Big Tech) that Mercor, Scale AI, Surge AI, and Handshake AI sell to, and it has no publicized referral/data-partnership-bonus program comparable to Mercor's ($100K) or Scale AI's ($10K) — see `SELLING-STRATEGY.md`.

**But a plausible partner for a narrower supply relationship**, specifically because of the SME-on-demand program and the cleared-federal-teams capability — an existing, real channel that the other four companies in this vault visibly lack.
