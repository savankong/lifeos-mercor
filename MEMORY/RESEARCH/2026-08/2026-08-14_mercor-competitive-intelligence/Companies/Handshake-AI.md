\# Handshake (Handshake AI)

**Status:** RESEARCHED
**Category:** Company — Core Competitor
**Value:** HIGH

## Overview

Handshake is the well-known university career-services platform (founded 2014 by three Michigan Tech students, incl. CEO **Garrett Lord**) that "re-founded" itself in **January 2025** by standing up a new internal business unit, Handshake AI, to sell expert-level human feedback (math, physics, computer science, and other specialist domains) to frontier labs including OpenAI and Anthropic. Lord has said he expected the AI unit to overtake the decade-old core recruiting business. *(HIGH — Wikipedia verified via fetch; AI-native GTM/Dealroom/Lenny's Newsletter pieces found via search corroborate the pivot narrative, not independently fetched)*

## Scale & Financials

- AI unit revenue: ~$5–10M at January 2025 launch → **$100M annualized within 8 months** → **~$1B gross annualized AI-training revenue by April 2026** (up from ~$550M) per aggregator reporting.
- Experts on the AI-training side reportedly paid **$100–125/hour**.

## Talent Market Focus

Handshake AI's entire competitive edge is its **pre-existing university pipeline**: it is the career-services platform for **92% of the top 500 U.S. universities** (1,500–1,600 university partnerships), giving it direct access to a claimed **18–20M+ students and alumni**, including **3M+ graduate-degree holders and 500,000+ PhDs** (50,000+ in STEM fields specifically). Unlike Mercor/Surge/Micro1 (mid-to-senior credentialed professionals recruited from the open market) or Turing (global engineering talent cloud), Handshake's talent is explicitly **students, new graduates, and graduate-level scholars** — its pitch to talent is "better pay than a campus job, real resume experience," and its pitch to employers/labs is "pre-verified academically-credentialed pool without building your own student pipeline." *(HIGH — verified via direct fetch of joinhandshake.com/ai/labs/)*

**Positioning vs. the field:** this is the one company in the set that didn't have to build a talent-sourcing channel from scratch for the AI-data pivot — it already owned the largest early-career/graduate-scholar distribution network in U.S. higher education and simply pointed a new product at it. That is both Handshake's structural advantage (near-zero incremental talent-acquisition cost) and a natural ceiling (the pool skews toward newly-credentialed graduate-level knowledge rather than the decades of applied professional judgment Mercor and Surge specifically recruit for — e.g., Mercor's ex-investment-banker/consultant angle, or Surge's Supreme-Court-clerk/C-suite bar).

## Relevance to Mercor

Handshake AI is a notable structural outlier: it's a 2014-founded, previously-unrelated company that pivoted an *existing* large user base (18M+ students/alumni, 550K+ employer relationships) into an AI-data business almost overnight — a different playbook from Mercor's ground-up marketplace build. If its AI-unit revenue claims hold up, it's growing faster off a smaller base than Mercor did at the same stage, which is a competitive signal worth tracking closely.
