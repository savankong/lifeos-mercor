## Overview

Third-party market-research estimates for the "AI training dataset market" vary widely by methodology — figures found range from **~$3.4–11.9B in 2026 growing to $16–50B by the early 2030s** (CAGR estimates 20–33% depending on the firm) — so treat any single headline market-size number as directional, not precise. *(CONFLICT — four different research firms (Cognitive Market Research, Grand View Research, Fortune Business Insights, Straits Research / Intel Market Research) gave four different figures in the same search pass)*

**Iteration 2 update:** independently fetched the **Fortune Business Insights** report directly (the only one of the four that resolved — Grand View Research returned 403). It puts the market at **$3.59B in 2025 → $4.44B in 2026 → $23.18B by 2034**, a 22.90% CAGR over 2026–2034, scoped by data type (text/audio/image/video), deployment mode, and end-user industry. *(HIGH for this specific figure, verified via direct fetch.)* This sits at the low end of the range other firms quoted for 2026 — a real methodological split appears to exist between narrower "raw training dataset" market definitions (Fortune Business Insights' scope) and broader definitions that may fold in human-labor/RLHF-services spend (which is arguably the more relevant boundary for comparing against Mercor/Surge/Turing revenue, since those companies sell labor-intensive services, not packaged datasets). The CONFLICT tag stays: this is a genuine definitional disagreement across the industry, not a research gap that more searching would resolve.

More directionally reliable: each frontier AI lab reportedly now spends **on the order of $1B/year on human training data**, and once post-training, evaluation, and RL data are included, the "human-data economy" is described as an order of magnitude larger than the dataset-market line item alone suggests. A structural scarcity argument underlies the whole category: the usable stock of quality human public text (~300 trillion tokens) is projected to be substantially consumed by 2026–2032, and synthetic data is hitting overfitting/diminishing-returns limits — pushing labs toward paying a premium for **expert-generated data that doesn't exist anywhere online** (i.e., exactly Mercor's and Micro1's product). *(MED — synthesized claim from search-result summaries, not independently fetched from a primary research report; directionally consistent with the customer-concentration and revenue-growth data independently verified in the Mercor and competitor company files)*
