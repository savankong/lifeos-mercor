\# Summary — Mercor Competitive Intelligence (Pass 4)

*2026-08-14. Read `INDEX.md` first for full navigation; this file is the short version.*

**Mercor** is the fastest-growing player in the AI expert-data / human-feedback market — from ~$75M ARR (Feb 2025) to a reported ~$2B annualized run rate (mid-2026) — built on connecting vetted domain experts (doctors, lawyers, PhDs, engineers) to frontier AI labs for RLHF, SFT, and reasoning-evaluation data. Its 22-year-old founders (Brendan Foody, Adarsh Hiremath, Surya Midha) became some of the youngest self-made billionaires in the world when the company hit a $10B valuation in October 2025; a further raise at a reported $20B valuation was in early-stage talks as of July 2026.

**Most of that growth is a byproduct of one external shock, not pure category creation:** Meta's $14.3B stake in Scale AI (June 2025) broke Scale's neutrality with rival AI labs, and the resulting exodus of spend to "neutral" vendors — Surge AI, Mercor, Micro1, Handshake AI, Turing, Invisible Technologies, Snorkel — is what actually re-shaped this market. Mercor's specific edge within that reshuffle is the credentialed-expert niche; Surge AI (bootstrapped, no outside investor) arguably has the strongest neutrality claim of anyone in the set.

**Two 2026 events are the most important due-diligence flags found in this pass:** a March 2026 supply-chain data breach that cost Mercor its Meta contract and exposed up to 4TB of data, and a governance/self-dealing flag around the Deeptune acquisition (CEO Foody personally invested in Deeptune before having Mercor acquire it three months later). Neither appears to have stopped Mercor's fundraising momentum, but both are directly relevant to any "is this durable" assessment.

**What this vault covers (2 passes):** Mercor deep profile; 6 competitor profiles (Scale AI, Surge AI, Micro1, Handshake AI, Turing, Invisible Technologies); 6 people; 3 investors; 3 products; 3 technologies; 3 trends. Both the breadth gate (≥3 researched entities per category) and the depth gate (every CRITICAL/HIGH entity researched or explicitly skipped) are met — see `ENTITIES.md` for the full status table.

**Pass 2 added two hard numbers that sharpen the story:** Scale AI's own guidance puts 2026 revenue at ~$1B, down from ~$2B in 2025 — the clearest quantified evidence of how much the Meta deal cost Scale and handed to Mercor and peers. And Turing, the most capital-efficient of the venture-backed competitors ($300M blended ARR on only $225M raised), turns out not to be a clean apples-to-apples comparison to Mercor since its ARR blends legacy talent-hiring revenue with AI-data revenue.

**Pass 4 — talent-market segmentation.** The seven companies in this vault look like they compete head-to-head, but they actually source talent from largely different pools: Mercor, Surge, and Micro1 all recruit mid-to-senior credentialed professionals from the open market (and so compete directly for the same people); Handshake AI instead owns a university pipeline (92% of the top 500 US schools) and sources students/new grads/graduate scholars; Turing runs on geographic labor arbitrage (global engineering talent, emerging-market-heavy); Scale AI's original model is mass-market gig recruiting (Outlier/Remotasks, $20-40/hr) rather than credential selectivity; Invisible Technologies doesn't brand a talent tier at all, selling process maturity instead. See the new comparison table in `LANDSCAPE.md`. This reframes the competitive set: Handshake and Turing look less like direct Mercor competitors and more like parallel bets on different labor markets entirely.

**Pass 3 — Mercor business-model deep dive.** Mercor is not one product but four: the core RLHF/SFT/rubrics/RL-environments/APEX suite; a Forward-Deployed-Engineer-style **Enterprise AI** consulting arm (high-touch B2B, no public pricing); and a **Data monetization** product that pays enterprises (up to $100K referral bonuses) to license internal Slack/email/CRM/code data to AI labs. The core marketplace runs on a **~35% take rate** with contractors keeping 60–70% of client billing — applying that rate to the ~$2B mid-2026 gross run-rate implies **net revenue closer to $600–700M/yr**, well below the headline gross figure most press coverage cites. GTM was historically sales-team-free (founder-led, word-of-mouth) for the marketplace, but Enterprise AI and Data both run conventional high-touch/referral B2B motions — a real divergence in how the company sells depending on which product line. A new, dated, verified risk item: a November 2025 incident where a cancelled project ("Musen") was replaced with a lower-paying one ("Nova," $16/hr vs. $21/hr) for the same contractor pool — the first concrete instance of the previously-vague "declining pay" press characterization.
